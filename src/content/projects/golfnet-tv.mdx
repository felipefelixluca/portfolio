## Brief

A mobile streaming app for live golf, where the defining product idea was running **two concurrent live channels** the viewer can switch between with a swipe. The product hinges on getting that gesture right: discoverable on first launch, then invisible afterwards.

## Process

- **Tutorial-led onboarding** — splash + a single coachmark screen explaining the 1CH/2CH swipe with a clear hand gesture and dimmed live feed behind. The tutorial appears once, then never again.
- **Player layout** — live video pinned to the top, concurrent schedule below. The schedule density is intentionally high; this is a viewer who knows what they're looking for.
- **Library** — VOD as a vertical list with consistent thumbnail + title + duration formatting. Episode covers do the work; chrome stays out of the way.
- **Settings & login** — kept conservative; this is a video app and the surface area outside the player should be small.

<Callout title="Onboard the gesture once, then disappear" accent="red">
Gesture-led navigation is a UX hazard when overdone, but it's the right call here because the two-channel model *is* the product. The coachmark earns its keep on first launch; everything after that trusts the viewer.
</Callout>

## Outcome

A first-launch flow that makes the differentiator (concurrent channels) immediately discoverable, with a player surface that gets out of the way for repeat viewing.
