## Brief

NRT's original transit programme had two real problems: **local visibility** on the web was poor, and the **mobile experience** for the people most likely to use it — travellers between flights with a phone in hand — wasn't there. The brief was to modernise the service surface and add an online booking system that could actually be completed in transit.

## Process

- **User interviews** with local tourists at the airport, to understand the real expectations and friction of "I have six hours, what can I do" decision-making.
- **Wireframes and responsive layouts** for the new pages, based on the research.
- For the landing page, **two patterns built and run as an A/B test** rather than picked by committee — the experimental design baked into the launch plan.
- A new **booking flow** — destination + people + date + meeting time, with a calendar pattern designed to work on a phone you're holding with one hand on a train.

<Callout title="Two landing variants, not one chosen winner" accent="blue">
Building both patterns and shipping them as a live A/B test cost slightly more design time, but removed the most expensive failure mode for a redesigned landing page: launching the wrong one with no data.
</Callout>

## Outcome

A modernised, mobile-first service surface with a booking system that fits the in-transit use case, plus a launch plan that produced real data on which landing pattern converted better.
