## Brief

JCLA's member site had accumulated. Each new programme — events, study mail magazines, video service, cosmetic-concierge curriculum, jobs board, member-only downloads — had been added as its own branch, and members were expected to know which branch to look under. Most of the content was member-only, and the member gate was repeated up and down the tree.

The brief was to make the member area **usable on the first visit** and to simplify the surface enough that maintaining it didn't require keeping the whole map in your head.

## Process

The first artifact was a sitemap of what existed. The pattern fell out of the diagram almost immediately: the page graph was wide, the member gate was leaking into every branch, and roughly two-thirds of the leaves were variations on the same destination.

<CosmeticsSitemap />

From there, the IA work was straightforward: identify the genuine destinations (eight), make them direct children of a single side menu, and pull the member gate up to one place — the side menu itself.

The UI and form redesign followed the same logic. The registration form went from a single long page to a four-step process with inline validation; the desktop top page surfaces news, calendar, and topics in a single scan; the mobile top is a faithful one-column reflow without dropping density.

<Callout title="One member gate, not six" accent="yellow">
Pulling the gate up to the side menu meant every member-only page could drop its own gating logic. Smaller change for engineering, simpler mental model for members, and dramatically easier to add new member-only content later without re-litigating the access pattern.
</Callout>

## Outcome

A site that members can navigate on their first visit, with maintenance load proportional to the number of destinations (eight) rather than the number of leaves (30+). Form completion improved in qualitative testing once steps and validation replaced the long single page.
